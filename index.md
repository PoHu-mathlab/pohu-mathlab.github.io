***Exact Global Modes of Two-Term Weighted Sums of i.i.d. Geometric Random Variables***
5 pages
Let X and Y be independent geometric random variables on Z≥0 with P(X = k) =P(Y = k) =p(1 − p)^k. For positive integers a, b, we determine the global modes of Z = aX + bY . The probability mass function may have gaps and local rises and falls. We prove that every global mode is a multiple of lcm(a, b) and that there are at most two global modes. A logarithmic threshold gives their exact locations and determines whether one or two modes occur. We also give an exact formula for every probability and a small-p asymptotic formula for the mode. The proof parametrizes the solutions of ax + by = n by residue classes and then compares the maxima of consecutive blocks.
**Category:** Probability

***Ruin, Survival, and Arbitrarily High Finite Targets in a +2/ − 1 Gambling Game***
10 pages
In each round, a gambler either wins two units or loses one unit. The game ends when the capital first reaches zero. Starting with one unit, what is the probability of reaching a very large but finite target? For independent fair rounds, this probability tends to (3 − √5)/2, not to zero. The ruin probability is the inverse golden ratio.
We first use path counting to show that ruin is not certain. The strong Markov property then gives q_(x+y) = q_x q_y, so the ruin probability from x units is q_x = ((√5 − 1)/2)^x.
For a general win probability p, the critical value is p = 1/3.
We then keep the stationary win rate at 1/2, but allow the outcomes to follow a symmetric two-state Markov chain. Let θ be the probability that the next outcome repeats the previous one. The survival probability is
1 − [(1 + r + r²)/(2(1 + r))] r^(x−1),where r = [√((1−θ)² + 4θ²) − (1−θ)]/(2θ).
It decreases strictly with θ, although the stationary win rate and the mean increment do not change. As θ → 1, the survival probability tends to 1/4, but at θ = 1 it is 1/2. Thus the target limit and the persistence limit do not commute.
**Category:** Probability
